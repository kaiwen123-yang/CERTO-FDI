\begin{corollary}[Cost of a fixed diagnostic endpoint]
\label{sf:fixed-endpoint-cor}
Retain the fixed-controller experiment of Theorem~\ref{sf:two-regime}
and the fixed-calendar risk interface of Section~\ref{sf:risk}.
Fix a miss tolerance $\varepsilon_{\rm m}\in(0,1)$ and let
$0<\alpha<1-\varepsilon_{\rm m}$. Write
\begin{equation}\label{sf:endpoint-threshold}
\Lambda_\alpha=\tfrac12
 \bigl(z_{1-\alpha}+z_{1-\varepsilon_{\rm m}}\bigr)^2,
\qquad z_q=\Phi^{-1}(q),
\end{equation}
where $\Phi$ is the standard normal distribution function. Define
\begin{equation}\label{sf:endpoint-oracle}
H_{\rm or}(\alpha)=\min\{H\in\mathbb N_{\ge1}:O_H\ge\Lambda_\alpha\}.
\end{equation}
Let $H_{\rm diag}(\alpha)$ be the smallest integer endpoint for which
some legal deterministic calendar and a test of its declared retained states
have uniform false-alarm probability at most $\alpha$ and uniform power at
least $1-\varepsilon_{\rm m}$. The controller, horizon, calendar and test
are selected before the innovations. The oracle is the ideal
healthy-subtracted complete white record, not the composite full-record
experiment.

If $\beta_k>0$, put
$C=(\sqrt2/5)\sqrt{F\beta_k r}\,\eta^{3/2}$. As $\alpha\downarrow0$,
\begin{equation}\label{sf:endpoint-positive}
\begin{aligned}
&H_{\rm diag}-H_{\rm or}\\
&\quad=\frac{2C}{F\eta^2}\sqrt{H_{\rm or}}+o(\sqrt{H_{\rm or}})\\
&\quad=\frac{2\sqrt2}{5}\sqrt{\frac{\beta_k r}{F\eta}}
   \sqrt{H_{\rm or}}\\
&\qquad+o(\sqrt{H_{\rm or}}).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
If $\beta_k=0$, then
\begin{equation}\label{sf:endpoint-zero}
0\le H_{\rm diag}-H_{\rm or}=O(1).
\end{equation}
No positive lower constant or $\Theta(1)$ endpoint excess is asserted.

For the common-positive-definite-noise compact controller family of
Theorem~\ref{sf:uniform}, define $H_{\rm joint}$ by also permitting the
known controller to be selected from $\mathcal K$ before the data. If
$C_{\inf}=\inf_{K\in\mathcal K}C(K)$, then
\begin{equation}\label{sf:endpoint-joint}
\begin{aligned}
&H_{\rm joint}-H_{\rm or}\\
&\quad=\frac{2C_{\inf}}{F\eta^2}\sqrt{H_{\rm or}}
  +o(\sqrt{H_{\rm or}}).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
This uses an infimum and does not assume continuity of $k(K)$ or attainment
of a controller/calendar supremum. These are deterministic endpoint costs,
not sequential stopping times, average run lengths, or expected stopping times.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}\leavevmode
For each fixed calendar, Section~\ref{sf:risk} gives existence of the
required test exactly when $G_{K,H}(\pi)\ge\Lambda_\alpha$.
Taking $z=0$ in the information infimum and using projection contraction
gives $0\le G_{K,H}(\pi)\le O_H$. Thus no endpoint below
$H_{\rm or}$ is feasible, for either the fixed controller or the common-oracle
joint design. We do not identify existence at a horizon with a possibly
unattained equality $\sup_\pi G_{K,H}(\pi)=\Lambda_\alpha$.

\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Oracle increments and integer rounding.}
Put $A_{\rm or}=F\eta^2/6$ and $B_{\rm or}=F\eta^2/2=3A_{\rm or}$.
Summing the affine fault squares gives the exact polynomial
\begin{equation}\label{sf:endpoint-oracle-polynomial}
\begin{aligned}
O_H={}&A_{\rm or}H^3
 +\left(\frac{F\rho_0\eta}{2}+\frac{F\eta^2}{4}\right)H^2\\
&+\left(\frac{F\rho_0^2}{2}
 +\frac{F\rho_0\eta}{2}+\frac{F\eta^2}{12}\right)H.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The normal quantile in \eqref{sf:endpoint-threshold} tends to infinity,
so $\Lambda_\alpha\to\infty$ and $n:=H_{\rm or}(\alpha)\to\infty$.
By minimality of $n$, for sufficiently small $\alpha$,
\begin{equation}\label{sf:endpoint-overshoot}
\begin{aligned}
0\le O_n-\Lambda_\alpha
 &<O_n-O_{n-1}\\
 &=\tfrac F2(\rho_0+\eta n)^2=O(n^2).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The exact cubic also implies, uniformly for nonnegative integer
$h\le M\sqrt n$ with fixed $M<\infty$,
\begin{equation}\label{sf:endpoint-increment}
O_{n+h}-O_n=B_{\rm or}n^2h+O_M(n^2).
\end{equation}
Indeed the remainder is a fixed linear combination of
$nh^2,h^3,nh,h^2,h$. For any fixed integer $L\ge0$ the stronger form is
$O_{n+L}-O_n=B_{\rm or}Ln^2+O_L(n)$.

\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{All earlier endpoints in the positive branch.}
Let $G_H^*=\sup_\pi G_{K,H}(\pi)=O_H-D_{K,H}^*$ and
$d=C/B_{\rm or}$. The already proved limit implies
\begin{equation}\label{sf:endpoint-tail}
e_n:=\sup_{H\ge n}
\left|\frac{D_{K,H}^*}{H^{5/2}}-C\right|\longrightarrow0.
\end{equation}
Fix $\delta\in(0,d)$. For every integer
$H=n+h\le n+\lfloor(d-\delta)\sqrt n\rfloor$, the same tail error,
\eqref{sf:endpoint-overshoot} and \eqref{sf:endpoint-increment} give
\begin{equation}\label{sf:endpoint-lower}
\begin{aligned}
G_H^*-\Lambda_\alpha
 &\le B_{\rm or}(d-\delta)n^{5/2}+O(n^2)\\
 &\quad -(C-e_n)n^{5/2}\ <\ 0
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
for all sufficiently large $n$. Here $C-e_n>0$ eventually, and
$H^{5/2}\ge n^{5/2}$. This excludes every integer in that range;
all integers below $n$ were excluded by the oracle. No monotonicity of
$G_H^*$ has been assumed.

For an upper endpoint take
$H=n+\lceil(d+\delta)\sqrt n\rceil$ and the actual terminal-balanced
calendar with $\xi_*=2\beta_k\eta/(Fr)$. Its proved bound is
$D_{K,H}(\pi_H^{\xi_*})\le CH^{5/2}+M_KH^2$ for one fixed $M_K$.
Since $H^{5/2}=n^{5/2}+O_\delta(n^2)$, it follows that
\begin{equation}\label{sf:endpoint-upper}
\begin{aligned}
&G_{K,H}(\pi_H^{\xi_*})-\Lambda_\alpha\\
&\quad\ge B_{\rm or}\delta n^{5/2}+O_\delta(n^2)>0.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
This strict inequality provides a genuine calendar and hence a test,
without finite-horizon supremum attainment. It also proves that the defining
set for $H_{\rm diag}$ is nonempty. The lower and upper comparisons, followed
by $\delta\downarrow0$, prove \eqref{sf:endpoint-positive}; the second
coefficient there is the substitution of the given $C$.

\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{The score-preserving branch.}
The explicit profile-aware calendar in Appendix~\ref{sf:zero-proof},
rather than an unattained supremum, supplies a fixed $M_0<\infty$ with
$D_{K,H}(\pi_H^0)\le M_0H^2$ for all sufficiently large $H$.
Choose a fixed positive integer $L$ such that $B_{\rm or}L>M_0$.
At $H=n+L$,
\begin{equation}\label{sf:endpoint-zero-upper}
\begin{aligned}
G_{K,n+L}(\pi_{n+L}^0)-\Lambda_\alpha
 &\ge B_{\rm or}Ln^2-M_0(n+L)^2+O_L(n)\\
 &=(B_{\rm or}L-M_0)n^2+O_L(n)>0.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Together with the oracle exclusion this proves
$0\le H_{\rm diag}-H_{\rm or}\le L$ eventually. Integer oracle overshoot
has order $n^2$, the same order as the zero-branch information deficit;
it cannot be discarded to infer a positive endpoint excess.

\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Joint design.}
The compact common-SPD proof gives a positive common gap-penalty lower bound,
so $C_{\inf}>0$. Its uniform theorem gives
\[
\sup_{H\ge n,K\in\mathcal K}
\left|H^{-5/2}D_{K,H}^*-C(K)\right|\longrightarrow0.
\]
Consequently $D_H^{\rm joint}/H^{5/2}\to C_{\inf}$ with one tail error,
and the preceding all-earlier-endpoint lower comparison holds with
$C_{\inf}$ in place of $C$.
For any fixed $\delta>0$, choose a fixed controller $K_\delta$ with
$C(K_\delta)<C_{\inf}+B_{\rm or}\delta/2$, possible by the definition of
infimum. Use its explicit calendar at
$H=n+\lceil(C_{\inf}/B_{\rm or}+\delta)\sqrt n\rceil$.
The fixed-$K_\delta$ construction gives
\[
G_{K_\delta,H}(\pi_H^{\xi_{K_\delta}})-\Lambda_\alpha
 \ge \tfrac12B_{\rm or}\delta n^{5/2}+O_\delta(n^2)>0.
\]
Thus a controller/calendar/test exists for the upper comparison even if
the infimum is not attained. Letting $\delta\downarrow0$ after the risk limit
proves \eqref{sf:endpoint-joint}.
\end{proof}

\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Risk-scale form.}
The relation $\Lambda_\alpha\sim\log(1/\alpha)$ can be verified directly.
For $t>0$, write $\phi(t)=(2\pi)^{-1/2}e^{-t^2/2}$ and
$I(t)=\int_t^\infty\phi(u)\,du$. Since
$\int_t^\infty u\phi(u)\,du=\phi(t)$, $I(t)\le\phi(t)/t$.
Integration by parts gives
\[
I(t)=\frac{\phi(t)}t-\int_t^\infty\frac{\phi(u)}{u^2}\,du
 \ge\frac{\phi(t)}t-\frac{I(t)}{t^2}.
\]
Hence $t\phi(t)/(t^2+1)\le I(t)\le\phi(t)/t$.
For $t=z_{1-\alpha}\to\infty$, logarithms yield
$\log(1/\alpha)=t^2/2+\log t+O(1)\sim t^2/2$.
The fixed $z_{1-\varepsilon_{\rm m}}$ changes
$(t+z_{1-\varepsilon_{\rm m}})^2/2$ by only $O(t)$.
Equation~\eqref{sf:endpoint-oracle-polynomial} and the overshoot bound then give
\[
H_{\rm or}\sim
\left(\frac{6\log(1/\alpha)}{F\eta^2}\right)^{1/3}.
\]
The positive-branch excess therefore has order $(\log(1/\alpha))^{1/6}$.
This consequence requires fixed miss tolerance and the exact linear experiment;
it does not apply to the bounded-time nonlinear certificate domain.

\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Why a universal positive zero-branch excess fails.}
An already admissible score-recovering plant provides an exact illustration.
Take $A_K=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1/2\\0&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$,
$G=B=e_2$, $n_0=1$, $\rho_0=0$, $\eta=1$, $r=1/100$ and $k=1$;
both exact-one-slot transition profiles have $g=0$.
Here $F=1$ and $H_2=[e_1/2,e_2]$, so $P_2=I_2$ and $\beta_1=0$.
Use the post-input task pattern $+1,0,-1,0$ repeatedly, retaining the
nonzero-task states. If $H$ is even, replace its final missing slot by
holding the previous task, so $H$ is retained. All true spans have length
at most two, and $P_\pi=I$ on the complete input record, including the prefix.

Put $S_t=\sum_{j=0}^t g_j$. The prefix has $g_0=1$, and this calendar
has $|S_t|\le3$. With $\theta_j=j$, $\gamma_j=g_j$,
$N=\gamma^\top\theta$ and $D=\|\gamma\|^2\ge H/2$, Abel summation gives
$|N|\le6H$. Thus $\alpha_{\rm rep}=N/D$ has $|\alpha_{\rm rep}|\le12$.
The observable $v=\theta-\alpha_{\rm rep}\gamma$ has zero scalar healthy mass.
For $\lambda_j=g_jv_j$, its complete partial masses obey
$|\sum_{j=0}^t\lambda_j|\le18(t+1)$.
The same full-path support and observable dual as in
Appendix~\ref{sf:geometry} therefore give
\begin{equation}\label{sf:endpoint-rounding-example}
\begin{aligned}
D_{K,H}(\pi)
 &\le\frac{N^2}{2D}+r\sum_{t=0}^{H-1}18(t+1)\\
 &\le\frac9{100}H^2+\frac{3609}{100}H.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
For every integer $H\ge226$, this is strictly less than $H^2/4$.
Set $\Lambda_H=O_H-H^2/4=H^3/6+H/12$ and
$\alpha_H=1-\Phi(\sqrt{2\Lambda_H}-z_{1-\varepsilon_{\rm m}})$.
Then $\alpha_H\downarrow0$,
$O_{H-1}=O_H-H^2/2<\Lambda_H<O_H$, and the displayed calendar has
$G_{K,H}(\pi)>\Lambda_H$. Hence
$H_{\rm diag}(\alpha_H)=H_{\rm or}(\alpha_H)=H$.
The zero-branch excess vanishes along this sequence, despite its positive
quadratic information deficit. This rules out a universal $\Theta(1)$
or positive-constant refinement of \eqref{sf:endpoint-zero}.
