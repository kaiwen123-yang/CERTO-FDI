\paragraph{Frozen physical contract.}
The causal plant and feedback are
\[
x_j=\lambda x_{j-1}+u_j+\mathbf1_{\{h=1\}}a_j+g_jb_j^h+w_j,\qquad
u_j=-Kx_{j-1},\qquad c=\lambda-K,\quad |c|<1.
\]
The initial state $x_{-n_0}=0$ is known, $n_0\ge1$ fixed, and
$w_j$ are independent $N(0,q)$, $q>0$. The healthy prefix
$j=-n_0+1,\ldots,0$ has $a_j=0$, task $g=+1$, and all states are read.
Thereafter $a_j=\rho_0+\eta j$, $\rho_0\ge0$, $\eta>0$.
Each hypothesis chooses a full deterministic healthy path with unrestricted
level and rate at most $r/2$. Their actual difference class is
$\mathcal Z_H=\{z:|\Delta z_j|\le r\}$, $r>0$.
The known-horizon deterministic calendar reads every held state at task
$g=\pm1$ and excludes at least $k\ge1$ slots per change.
On excluded slots $g_j$ is known and $|g_j|\le1$.
The diagnostic protocol does not read fast internal states or control
commands. No state, innovation or healthy level is reset.
All parameters are fixed. There are no actuator, tracking or state-amplitude
constraints in this first analytic contract; adding such constraints requires
a separate feasibility argument.

\paragraph{Exact experiment.}
For retained times $t_i$, set $t_0=-n_0$ and $m_i=t_i-t_{i-1}$.
The true conditional innovation satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:conditional}
x_{t_i}-c^{m_i}x_{t_{i-1}}
=\sum_{j=t_{i-1}+1}^{t_i}c^{t_i-j}(a_j^h+g_jb_j^h+w_j).
\end{equation}
Its noise variance is $qB_{m_i}$,
$B_m=\sum_{\ell=0}^{m-1}c^{2\ell}$.
These sums use disjoint original innovations. Let $T$ have unit rows
$c^{t_i-j}/\sqrt{B_{m_i}}$ on their corresponding input intervals,
and zero otherwise. Then $TT^\top=I$ and $P=T^\top T$ is an orthogonal
projection in the complete input space, with zero columns for any uncovered
terminal inputs. The full input difference is $a-gz$, so
\[
G_H(\pi)=\frac1{2q}\inf_{z\in\mathcal Z_H}\|P(a-gz)\|^2,\qquad
O_H=\frac1{2q}\sum_{j=1}^H a_j^2,\qquad D_H^*=O_H-\max_\pi G_H(\pi).
\]
The conditional predictor in \eqref{eq:conditional} uses the actual preceding
retained state, not an independent initialization. The joint output
covariance is still $qRF_cF_c^\top R^\top$, including its cross-block entries.
The full experiment is transformed by the common invertible map $F_c^{-1}$
to input innovations; fault, health and noise all transform together.
Thus the complete experiment and its oracle are control-neutral.

\begin{theorem}[Matching controlled-memory deficit]\label{thm:main}
Fix the preceding contract and let $m=k+1$,
\[
A_m=\sum_{\ell=0}^{m-1}c^\ell,\qquad
\kappa_c(k)=m-\frac{A_m^2}{B_m}>0.
\]
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sharp}
D_H^*=\frac{\sqrt2}{5q}\sqrt{\kappa_c(k)r}\,\eta^{3/2}H^{5/2}
+o(H^{5/2}).
\end{equation}
For every fixed $\xi>0$, the terminal-balanced symmetric calendar below has
\begin{equation}\label{eq:upper}
D_H(\pi_H^\xi)\le\frac1{10q}
\left(\frac{2\kappa_c(k)\eta^2}{\sqrt\xi}+r\eta\sqrt\xi\right)H^{5/2}
+O(H^2),
\end{equation}
and $\xi_*=2\kappa_c(k)\eta/r$ matches the converse coefficient.
Only the leading deficit coefficient is sharp.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
We keep the same full input-health class in both bounds.

\paragraph{Gap loss and uniform affine cross.}
A gap of $\ell$ missing state outputs creates one input interval
of length $m=\ell+1$. With $w_j=c^{m-j}$, $j=1,\ldots,m$, its full-minus-retained
squared-input-information loss is
\[
L_m(x)=x^\top M_mx,\qquad M_m=I-ww^\top/B_m\succeq0,\quad \|M_m\|\le1.
\]
For constant input $A$ it is $\kappa_c(\ell)A^2$.
Strict Cauchy--Schwarz gives $\kappa_c(\ell)>0$ for $\ell\ge1$ since $c\ne1$.
Nested deletion of state outputs in one initialized plant gives its
monotonicity in $\ell$. Also $\kappa_c(\ell)/\ell\to1$, hence
\[
b_c=\inf_{\ell\ge k}\kappa_c(\ell)/\ell>0.
\]
One explicit tail argument uses
$\kappa_c(\ell)\ge\ell+1-(1-|c|)^{-2}$ and then a finite positive prefix.

For affine input centered on this interval, set
$h_j=j-(m+1)/2$, $x=A\mathbf1+\eta h$. Unlike stationary M0, reflection does
not remove the cross term. Instead define
\[
L_{1,m}=\mathbf1^\top M_mh
=-\frac{A_m}{B_m}\sum_jh_jc^{m-j},\qquad L_{2,m}=h^\top M_mh\ge0.
\]
Since $B_m\ge1$, $|A_m|\le(1-|c|)^{-1}$ and $|h_j|\le m/2$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cross}
L_m(x)=\kappa_c(\ell)A^2+2\eta A L_{1,m}+\eta^2L_{2,m}
\ge\kappa_c(\ell)A^2-\frac{\eta A m}{(1-|c|)^2}.
\end{equation}
The input intervals are disjoint and their lengths sum to at most $H$,
while each center amplitude is at most $\rho_0+\eta H$. Thus their cumulative
negative cross costs at most
\begin{equation}\label{eq:error}
E_H=\frac{\eta(\rho_0+\eta H)H}{(1-|c|)^2}=O(H^2)
\end{equation}
uniformly over every allowed calendar.
Each missing amplitude is at most twice its interval-center amplitude, so
for the sum $T$ of actual gap norm losses,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dead}
T\ge\frac{b_c}{4}\sum_{\text{missing }j}a_j^2-E_H.
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Calendar-uniform converse.}
Terminal moves without a later read are dominated by staying and reading:
the old observations have the same laws and the new experiment contains them.
The oracle is unchanged. Thus restrict the optimal calendar to $t_N=H$.
Prefix states are all retained, so every non-singleton input interval is
post-onset and internal.

Fix $\varepsilon\in(0,1)$ and partition
$[\lceil\varepsilon H\rceil,H]$ into complete macros of length
$L=\lfloor H^{3/4}\rfloor$. On each clipped observed run of length $n$, choose
$z_i=g_i r\min(i,n-1-i)$, $i=0,\ldots,n-1$.
Set $z=0$ on missing inputs, run endpoints, prefix, macro joins and unused tail.
This gives one rate-$r$ full path, realized by the two paths $z/2,-z/2$.
Writing $h=gz$, the residual equals $a$ throughout each non-singleton interval,
including its right retained input. By orthogonal decomposition,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:adversary}
2qD_H(\pi)\ge \sum_j(2a_jh_j-h_j^2)+T.
\end{equation}
There is no stationary endpoint correction and no affine replacement of
the zero-target prefix.

In a late macro let $A$ be its minimum amplitude, $d$ missing slots,
$M$ clipped observed runs, and $n_i$ their lengths. Put
$C=r(2A-rL/2)/4>0$. Tent area and height give
$\sum(2ah-h^2)\ge C\sum n_i^2-2CL$.
There are $M-1$ genuine complete gaps within that macro, each of length at
least $k$ and center amplitude at least $A$.
With $M=0$ the resulting negative bound remains valid.
Equations \eqref{eq:cross}--\eqref{eq:dead} give two bounds on the same global $T$:
\[
T\ge\sum_{\rm macros}\kappa_c(k)A^2(M-1)-E_H,\qquad
T\ge\frac{b_c}{4}\sum_{\rm macros}A^2d-E_H.
\]
A crossing gap appears in no complete-gap count, but its missing nodes are
assigned once in the dead-budget sum.

Fix $\theta\in(0,1)$ and split $T=(1-\theta)T+\theta T$ before applying the
two bounds. Put $W=(1-\theta)\kappa_c(k)$.
For $M>0$, Cauchy and AM--GM bound a macro contribution by
\[
C(L-d)^2/M+WA^2M+\theta b_cA^2d/4-WA^2-2CL
\ge2A\sqrt{CW}(L-d)+\theta b_cA^2d/4-WA^2-2CL.
\]
Because $C\le rA/2$, $A\ge32Wr/(\theta^2b_c^2)$ suffices to absorb the
missing-slot subtraction uniformly. If $M=0,d=L$, the dead term alone gives
the same final bound $2A\sqrt{CW}L-WA^2-2CL$.
Late $A=\Theta(H)$ makes this valid for all calendars for sufficiently large
$H$, at fixed $\varepsilon,\theta$.
Summing gives leading term
$\sqrt{2rW}\sum A^{3/2}L$; macro coefficient, rounding and terminal Riemann
errors are $O(H^{9/4})$, $\sum A^2=O(H^{9/4})$, $\sum CL=O(H^2)$,
and the affine cross error is \eqref{eq:error}.
The Riemann sum equals
$(2/5)\eta^{3/2}(1-\varepsilon^{5/2})H^{5/2}+o(H^{5/2})$.
Take the minimum calendar deficit before the limit, then
$\varepsilon,\theta\downarrow0$, to obtain
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lower}
\liminf_{H\to\infty}D_H^*/H^{5/2}
\ge\frac{\sqrt2}{5q}\sqrt{\kappa_c(k)r}\,\eta^{3/2}.
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Terminal-balanced calendar and genuine input blocks.}
A symmetric block with $n=2m$ contains $m$ reads at $+$, $k$ missing slots,
$n$ reads at $-$, $k$ missing slots and $m$ reads at $+$, duration $2(n+k)$.
With budget $H-1$, fit the maximal number of blocks with
$\bar n_\ell=2\lceil\xi\ell/2\rceil$. Distribute remaining multiples of four
slots evenly, increasing $n_\ell$ by two per unit, with a fixed left-to-right
tie rule. Observe the remaining one to four tail states at $+$.
Then
\[
M=\sqrt{H/\xi}+O(1),\quad n_\ell=\xi\ell+O(1),\quad
A_\ell=\rho_0+\eta(x_\ell+c_\ell)=\eta\xi\ell^2+O(\ell+1),
\]
where $c_\ell=(2(n_\ell+k)+1)/2$.
Every padding increment is uniformly $O(1)$.
The preceding and succeeding states at an artificial block join are real
retained states; its next conditional interval is a singleton.
Consequently the input projection splits into these genuine interval blocks.
It does not make the state output covariance block diagonal.

\paragraph{Auxiliary vector and exact weighted-mass repair.}
On an active block, $A\ge r(k+n-1)/2$, use the local levels
\[
z_i^{+,L}=r(c_0+m-i),\quad
z_i^-=-r[c_0+\min(i-1,n-i)],\quad
z_i^{+,R}=r(c_0+i-1),\qquad c_0=(k+1)/2.
\]
Set the auxiliary input $U_i^0=A-g_i z_i^{loc}$ on held inputs and $U_i^0=A$
on missing inputs. Set $U^0=0$ on inactive blocks, prefix and tail.
Only finitely many early blocks can be inactive.
The baseline $\lambda_i^0$ is $g_iU_i^0$ on holds and zero on missing inputs.
It has exact zero sum, and its full-input support is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:baseline}
h_\ell^0=\frac{Ar}{2}n(n+2k)-4r^2\sum_{j=0}^{n/2-1}(c_0+j)^2.
\end{equation}
Indeed its partial sums are nonnegative on the left and nonpositive on the
right, reach zero at the negative-run center and at the block end, and saturate
$\Delta z^{loc}=-r\Delta t\,\operatorname{sign}(Q)$ whenever $Q\ne0$.
Zero weights in missing slots only create the correct physical-time plateau.
Summation by parts and the fourfold multiplicity of local levels prove
\eqref{eq:baseline}. Local levels are used to generate a dual, not asserted
to join into a feasible global primal.

Let $P_\ell$ be the genuine input projector of this block and set
\[
p_\ell=P_\ell g_\ell,\quad N_\ell=g_\ell^\top P_\ell U_\ell^0,\quad
D_\ell=\|p_\ell\|^2,\quad
\alpha_\ell=N_\ell/D_\ell,\quad
v_\ell=P_\ell U_\ell^0-\alpha_\ell p_\ell.
\]
The $2n-2$ held inputs not ending a non-singleton interval have singleton
projectors and $g_i^2=1$, so $D_\ell\ge2n-2\ge n$.
The difference $gP_\ell U^0-\lambda^0$ lies in just two fixed spans of length
$k+1$. In each span $\|P U^0\|_1\le(k+1)A$ and the baseline contributes
at most $A$. Thus with $C_k=2(k+2)$,
\[
|N_\ell|\le C_kA,\quad
|\alpha_\ell|\le C_kA/n,\quad
g_\ell^\top v_\ell=0,\quad P_\ell v_\ell=v_\ell,\quad
\|v_\ell-P_\ell U_\ell^0\|^2=N_\ell^2/D_\ell\le C_k^2A^2/n.
\]
These assertions require no sign assumption on $c$ or on projected vectors.

Write $\delta\lambda_\ell=g_\ell v_\ell-\lambda_\ell^0$.
It has zero mass and, since $\|g_\ell p_\ell\|_1\le L_\ell$,
\[
\|\delta\lambda_\ell\|_1\le C_k(k+3)A.
\]
For the full rate class, support of any zero-mass vector in a contiguous
length-$L$ block is $r\sum|Q_j|\le r(L-1)\|\lambda\|_1/2$.
The correction costs $O(rA(n+k))$ per block, hence $O(H^2)$ in total.
The global $v$ is assembled from these input-range vectors and zero
prefix/tail vectors. It belongs to $\operatorname{range}P$ and has
$\sum g_jv_j=0$ exactly.
Support sublinearity and full-path restriction, not independent nuisance
resets, yield
\begin{equation}\label{eq:supportupper}
h_{\mathcal Z}(gv)\le\sum h_\ell^0+O(H^2)
\le\frac r2\sum A_\ell n_\ell^2+O(H^2).
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Dual energy and non-reflection oracle cost.}
The observable row direction is $Tv$, with variance $q\|v\|^2$.
Fenchel's inequality gives
$G\ge[a^\top v-\|v\|^2/2-h_{\mathcal Z}(gv)]/q$, and therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dualerror}
2qD_H(\pi)\le
\|(I-P)a\|^2+\|Pa-v\|^2+2h_{\mathcal Z}(gv).
\end{equation}
On active blocks $|a_i-U_i^0|\le(\eta+r)(n_\ell+k)$.
Inactive inputs cost $O(1)$, prefix errors are zero, and the at most four
tail errors are $O(H)$ each. Projection contraction and the repair bound give
\[
\|Pa-v\|^2\le2\|a-U^0\|^2+2C_k^2\sum A_\ell^2/n_\ell=O(H^2).
\]
The input centers of the two block gaps are offset from the block center by
$1/2\pm(n_\ell+k)/2$.
Write $\kappa=\kappa_c(k)$, $L_1=L_{1,k+1}$ and $L_2=L_{2,k+1}$.
Their exact total norm loss is
\[
2\kappa(A_\ell+\eta/2)^2+\frac{\kappa\eta^2}{2}(n_\ell+k)^2
+4\eta(A_\ell+\eta/2)L_1+2\eta^2L_2.
\]
It follows that
$\|(I-P)a\|^2=2\kappa\sum A_\ell^2+O(H^{3/2})$.
This expression retains the genuine nonzero affine cross.
Together with \eqref{eq:supportupper}--\eqref{eq:dualerror},
\[
2qD_H(\pi_H^\xi)\le2\kappa\sum A_\ell^2+r\sum A_\ell n_\ell^2+O(H^2).
\]
Finally $\sum_{\ell\le M}\ell^4=M^5/5+O(M^4)$ gives
$\sum A_\ell^2=\eta^2H^{5/2}/(5\sqrt\xi)+O(H^2)$ and
$\sum A_\ell n_\ell^2=\eta\sqrt\xi H^{5/2}/5+O(H^2)$.
This proves \eqref{eq:upper}. Optimizing at $\xi_*=2\kappa\eta/r$ matches
\eqref{eq:lower}, proving \eqref{eq:sharp}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Restricted known-gain coefficient comparison]
For $m=k+1$, $\kappa_c(k)$ is strictly decreasing on $[0,1)$, and
$\kappa_{-c}(k)>\kappa_c(k)$ for $0<c<1$.
Thus a specified finite known-gain family is compared by its $\kappa$ values.
If an additional admissible fixed stability-margin family is
$|\lambda-K|\le\bar c<1$, its leading deficit coefficient is minimized at
$c=\bar c$, $K=\lambda-\bar c$. This compares fixed-gain asymptotic
coefficients, not finite-horizon constrained-control risks.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For $0<c<1$,
$A_m^2/B_m=[(1+c)/(1-c)][(1-c^m)/(1+c^m)]$.
Its logarithmic derivative is
$2/(1-c^2)-2mc^{m-1}/(1-c^{2m})>0$ because
$B_m>mc^{m-1}$ by strict AM--GM.
For negative $c$, triangle inequality gives
$|A_m(-c)|<A_m(c)$ and $B_m(-c)=B_m(c)$.
Subtracting this ratio from $m$ proves both claims.
The comparisons keep the common external input and innovation model.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Evidence and remaining scope.}
The standard-library script
\texttt{outputs/m1\_controlled\_memory\_checks\_v1.py} and its JSON report
check 1270 initialized-state retained subsets against direct covariance
inversion, 129 complete repaired calendars, and 695 local repair/support
certificates. Gap profiles include zero, constant, linear and alternating
regressors. Calculations use $q=1,n_0=3$; arbitrary fixed parameters are
covered by the proof, not these finite cases.
This scalar proof remains subject to independent review.
Unknown plants, extra sensors or fast control-command access, measurement
noise, saturation, fixed amplitude boxes, adaptive horizons, and hardware
validity are separate problems. No general controller-optimality claim
or completed TAC research goal follows from this theorem.
