\paragraph{Frozen contract.}
Let
\[
x_j=A_Kx_{j-1}+B(a_j^h+g_jb_j^h)+G\xi_j,\qquad
A_K=A-B_uK,\qquad \xi_j\overset{\mathrm{iid}}{\sim}N(0,I_p).
\]
The dimension is fixed, $A_K$ is known and strictly stable,
$G\in\mathbb R^{d\times p}$ has full column rank, $1\le p\le d$,
and $0\ne B\in\operatorname{range}G$.
The known initial state is $x_{-n_0}=0$, $n_0\ge1$ fixed.
Prefix states are all read at $g=+1$, $a_j=0$; thereafter
$a_j=\rho_0+\eta j$, $\rho_0\ge0$, $\eta>0$.
Each hypothesis chooses its own unrestricted-level scalar healthy path
with rate at most $r/2$, so the difference class is
$\mathcal Z_H=\{z:|\Delta z_j|\le r\}$, $r>0$.
A deterministic known-horizon calendar reads full states at held tasks
$g=\pm1$ and excludes at least $k\ge1$ slots per change.
Missing $g_j$ are known with $|g_j|\le1$.
The attaining construction uses concatenable known exact-$k$ moves
in both directions. No plant, innovation or healthy level is reset.
Fast internal states and control commands are not diagnostic data.
No saturation or tracking-budget claim is made.

Put $f=G^\dagger B$, the unique preimage, and $F=\|f\|^2>0$.
Write $a_f=(f a_j)_j$, $H_gz=(f g_jz_j)_j$ and
\[
M_m=\sum_{r=0}^{m-1}A_K^rB,\quad
W_m=\sum_{r=0}^{m-1}A_K^rGG^\top(A_K^r)^\top,\quad
\beta_k=(k+1)F-M_{k+1}^\top W_{k+1}^\dagger M_{k+1}.
\]
The full-state oracle is $O_H=(F/2)\sum_{j=1}^H a_j^2$.
We use a supremum over admissible calendars, without assuming their
profile set is closed.

\begin{theorem}[Normalized penalty characterization]\label{thm:m2s}
For the frozen fixed-parameter contract, $\beta_k\ge0$.
If $\beta_k>0$,
\[
D_H^*=\frac{\sqrt2}{5}\sqrt{F\beta_kr}\,\eta^{3/2}H^{5/2}
+o(H^{5/2}),
\]
attained in leading coefficient by the terminal-balanced symmetric
construction at $\xi_*=2\beta_k\eta/(Fr)$.
If $\beta_k=0$, then $D_H^*/H^{5/2}\to0$.
This second assertion alone does not specify a lower exponent or a
sharp lower-order coefficient.
\end{theorem}

\paragraph{Common Gaussian support and the actual projector.}
The retained data have linear noise map
$L_R=RF_{A_K}\operatorname{diag}G$ and mean $L_R\theta$,
where $\theta=(f(a^h+gb^h))_j$.
Every parameter mean is in $\operatorname{range}L_R$, hence all Gaussian
supports coincide.
For a rank-$s$ SVD $L_R=U_s\Sigma_sV_s^\top$, the supported data are
equivalent to $\Sigma_s^{-1}U_s^\top Y$, with noise $N(0,I_s)$ and
mean $V_s^\top\theta$.
Thus pairwise KL is $\|V_s^\top\Delta\theta\|^2/2$.
A pseudoinverse formula without this image condition would not suffice.

The invertible triangular retained-state predictor maps the true noise
matrix to disjoint interval matrices
\[
H_m=[A_K^{m-1}G,\ldots,G],\qquad H_mH_m^\top=W_m.
\]
It subtracts $A_K^m$ times the actual preceding retained state, not a new
initial condition. Invertible left multiplication preserves rowspace.
Consequently the global input projector has blocks
$P_m=H_m^\top W_m^\dagger H_m$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:experiment}
G_H(\pi)=\tfrac12\inf_{z\in\mathcal Z_H}
\|P(a_f-H_gz)\|^2,\qquad P^2=P=P^\top.
\end{equation}
Rank-adapted independent Gaussian rows can be obtained with an orthonormal
basis $U_m$ of $\operatorname{range}W_m$:
$(U_m^\top W_mU_m)^{-1/2}U_m^\top H_m$.
There are $\operatorname{rank}W_m$ such rows, not $d$ independent unit rows.
The complete record recovers all latent inputs using
$G^\dagger(x_j-A_Kx_{j-1})$; its whole composite experiment and oracle are
control-neutral.

\paragraph{Uniform-in-span pseudoinverse bound for one fixed plant.}
Let
$\mathcal R_m=\operatorname{span}\{G,A_KG,\ldots,A_K^{m-1}G\}$.
Cayley--Hamilton makes $\mathcal R_d$ invariant and
$\mathcal R_m=\mathcal R_d$ for $m\ge d$.
For those $m$, $W_m\succeq W_d$ and their kernels coincide.
On $\mathcal R_d$ they are positive definite, so
$W_m^\dagger\preceq W_d^\dagger$ there, with both zero on the orthogonal kernel.
Hence
\[
\Gamma_K=\max_{1\le m\le d}\|W_m^\dagger\|<\infty,\qquad
\sup_m\|W_m^\dagger\|\le\Gamma_K.
\]
One must not bound all $W_m^\dagger$ by $W_1^\dagger$ before reachability
rank stabilization.
Strict stability also gives $S_B=\sum_{r\ge0}\|A_K^rB\|<\infty$.
Set $C_K=\Gamma_KS_B^2$.

\begin{proposition}[Recoverability and finite noise-free capacity]\label{prop:capacity}
Let $\nu_R$ be the first $m$ with $\mathcal R_m=\mathcal R_\infty$ and
$r_\infty=\dim\mathcal R_\infty$.
Then $\beta_k=0$ if and only if some $y$ satisfies
\[
G^\top(A_K^\top)^r y=f,\qquad r=0,\ldots,k.
\]
If $\beta_k=0$, then
$k<\nu_R\le r_\infty-p+1\le d-p+1$.
This is recovery of the constant scalar mean mode, not necessarily of
every latent input.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For $d_m=\mathbf1_m\otimes f$, $H_md_m=M_m$ and
$\beta_{m-1}=d_m^\top(I-P_m)d_m\ge0$.
It is zero exactly when $d_m$ belongs to $\operatorname{range}H_m^\top$,
which is the stated finite system.

Reachability rank starts at $p$, grows by at least one per step before
stabilization, and cannot resume growth after a plateau.
Thus $\nu_R\le r_\infty-p+1$.
If the finite system holds, set $\zeta=(A_K^\top-I)y$.
It annihilates $\mathcal R_k$.
If $k\ge\nu_R$, this invariant reached space is already saturated, so
$G^\top(A_K^\top)^r\zeta=0$ for every $r$.
The sequence $G^\top(A_K^\top)^r y$ would then be constantly equal to
nonzero $f$, whereas stability makes it tend to zero. This contradiction
proves the capacity bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:m2s}]
Nested state deletion shrinks the true noise-map rowspace, so
$\beta_\ell$ is nondecreasing. Since $\|M_m\|\le S_B$ and
$\|W_m^\dagger\|\le\Gamma_K$,
$\beta_\ell\ge(\ell+1)F-C_K$ and $\beta_\ell/\ell\to F$.
If $\beta_k>0$, then $b_K=\inf_{\ell\ge k}\beta_\ell/\ell>0$.

\paragraph{Affine gaps and a common full-history converse.}
For centered $h_j=j-(m+1)/2$, put $d_h=(h_jf)_j$ and
$N_m=H_md_h$.
The constant--slope loss coefficient is
$L_{1,m}=-M_m^\top W_m^\dagger N_m$, bounded in absolute value by $mC_K/2$;
the slope-square coefficient $L_{2,m}=d_h^\top(I-P_m)d_h$ is nonnegative.
Therefore an affine interval with center amplitude $A\ge0$ loses exactly
\[
\beta_{m-1}A^2+2\eta A L_{1,m}+\eta^2L_{2,m}
\ge\beta_{m-1}A^2-C_K\eta A m.
\]
Terminal moves without later readings are dominated by holding.
Prefix states are read, so all non-singleton spans are post-onset.
Their total length is at most $H$ and their amplitudes at most
$\rho_0+\eta H$. The total negative affine cross is consequently
$E_H=C_K\eta(\rho_0+\eta H)H=O(H^2)$, uniformly over calendars.

For $\beta_k>0$, divide the late interval into complete macros
of length $L=\lfloor H^{3/4}\rfloor$.
Use a scalar signed rate-$r$ tent on each clipped held run, zero at its
endpoints, on missing nodes, right-first reads, prefix, macro joins and unused tail.
This is one full path, realized by its two halves, and $h=gz$ vanishes
throughout every non-singleton input span.
If $T$ is its exact sum of gap norm losses,
\[
2D_H(\pi)\ge F\sum(2ah-h^2)+T.
\]
In a macro with minimum $A$, missing slots $d$, run count $M$ and lengths $n_i$,
tent saving is at least $C\sum n_i^2-2CL$,
$C=r(2A-rL/2)/4>0$.
Complete gaps within that macro give
$T\ge\sum\beta_kA^2(M-1)-E_H$.
Since missing amplitudes are at most twice their span-center amplitude,
the same $T$ also obeys $T\ge(b_K/4)\sum A^2d-E_H$.
Crossing gaps are not counted in the first bound but each missing node is
assigned once in the second.

Split this same global $T$ with fixed $\theta\in(0,1)$ and
$W=(1-\theta)\beta_k$. For $M>0$, Cauchy and AM--GM bound the macro contribution by
\[
FC(L-d)^2/M+WA^2M+\theta b_KA^2d/4-WA^2-2FCL
\ge2A\sqrt{FCW}(L-d)+\theta b_KA^2d/4-WA^2-2FCL .
\]
The condition $A\ge32FWr/(\theta^2b_K^2)$ absorbs $d$ uniformly.
For $M=0,d=L$, the dead term gives the same final bound without division.
With fixed late cutoff $\varepsilon>0$, $A=\Theta(H)$;
all coefficient, rounding, and incomplete Riemann-block remainders are
$O(H^{9/4})$, $\sum CL=O(H^2)$, and $E_H=O(H^2)$.
The leading sum is
$\sqrt{2FrW}(2/5)\eta^{3/2}(1-\varepsilon^{5/2})H^{5/2}$.
Take the calendar infimum before $H\to\infty$, then
$\varepsilon,\theta\downarrow0$, to obtain the asserted liminf coefficient.

\paragraph{An attaining dual for every rank and for $\beta_k\ge0$.}
For fixed $\xi>0$, symmetric blocks have even $n=2h$:
$h$ reads at $+$, $k$ missing slots, $n$ reads at $-$,
$k$ missing slots and $h$ reads at $+$.
With budget $H-1$, fit base $n_\ell=2\lceil\xi\ell/2\rceil$ and evenly distribute
remaining multiples of four slots. Observe the final one to four states.
Then
\[
M=\sqrt{H/\xi}+O(1),\quad n_\ell=\xi\ell+O(1),\quad
A_\ell=\eta\xi\ell^2+O(\ell+1).
\]
True state readings on both sides of an artificial join make its next input
interval a singleton. No output covariance has been reset.

On active blocks $A\ge r(k+n-1)/2$, use local levels with $c_0=(k+1)/2$:
\[
z_i^{+,L}=r(c_0+h-i),\quad
z_i^-=-r[c_0+\min(i-1,n-i)],\quad
z_i^{+,R}=r(c_0+i-1).
\]
Set $U_j=f(A-g_jz_j^{loc})$ on holds and $fA$ on missing inputs.
Inactive early blocks, prefix and tail have $U=0$.
Only uniformly finitely many blocks can be inactive.
The baseline scalar direction is $F(g_jA-z_j^{loc})$ on holds, zero otherwise.
It has zero mass and support
\[
h_\ell^0=F\left\{\frac{Ar}{2}n(n+2k)
-4r^2\sum_{j=0}^{n/2-1}(c_0+j)^2\right\}.
\]
Its partial sums have the correct left-positive/right-negative signs
and saturated local slopes; summation by parts proves this formula while
retaining the physical gap span $k+1$.
Local levels generate a dual, not a global primal path.

Let $h_\ell=(g_jf)_j$, $p_\ell=P_\ell h_\ell$,
$N_\ell=h_\ell^\top P_\ell U_\ell$,
$D_\ell=\|p_\ell\|^2$, and
$v_\ell=P_\ell U_\ell-(N_\ell/D_\ell)p_\ell$.
Full column rank of $G$ gives singleton $P_1=I_p$.
The $2n-2$ held singleton inputs therefore give $D_\ell\ge F(2n-2)\ge Fn$.
The discrepancy from the zero-mass baseline lies in just two fixed spans;
contraction bounds its scalar $L_1$ norm by $C_kFA$, $C_k=2(k+2)$.
Thus
\[
|N_\ell|\le C_kFA,\quad
h_\ell^\top v_\ell=0,\quad P_\ell v_\ell=v_\ell,\quad
\|v_\ell-P_\ell U_\ell\|^2=N_\ell^2/D_\ell\le C_k^2FA^2/n .
\]
For the corrected scalar weights
$\delta\lambda_j=h_j^\top v_j-\lambda_j^0$,
zero mass is exact and $\|\delta\lambda\|_1\le C_k(k+3)FA$.
The full scalar rate class has support $r\sum|Q_j|$;
a zero-mass length-$L$ vector costs at most $r(L-1)\|\delta\lambda\|_1/2$.
Full-path restriction and sublinearity yield
$h_{\mathcal Z}(\lambda)\le(Fr/2)\sum A_\ell n_\ell^2+O(H^2)$.

The direction is actually observable: on each interval use coefficient
$W_m^\dagger H_mv_m$ against the true retained predictor innovation.
Its latent noise direction is $H_m^\top W_m^\dagger H_mv_m=v_m$,
hence its variance is $\|v_m\|^2$.
Fenchel's inequality gives exactly
\[
2D_H(\pi)\le\|(I-P)a_f\|^2+\|Pa_f-v\|^2+2h_{\mathcal Z}(\lambda).
\]
Auxiliary errors and repairs cost $O(H^2)$:
$\|Pa_f-v\|^2\le2\|a_f-U\|^2+2C_k^2F\sum A_\ell^2/n_\ell$.
The two input gap centers have offsets $1/2\pm(n+k)/2$.
Their exact oracle norm loss is
\[
2\beta_k(A_\ell+\eta/2)^2+\frac{\beta_k\eta^2}{2}(n_\ell+k)^2
+4\eta(A_\ell+\eta/2)L_{1,k+1}+2\eta^2L_{2,k+1}.
\]
Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:upper}
D_H(\pi_H^\xi)\le\frac1{10}
\left(\frac{2\beta_k\eta^2}{\sqrt\xi}+Fr\eta\sqrt\xi\right)H^{5/2}
+O_\xi(H^2).
\end{equation}
Power sums of the block scales establish this bound for $\beta_k\ge0$.

For $\beta_k>0$, optimizing $\xi$ matches the converse.
For $\beta_k=0$, $0\le D_H^*$ and every fixed $\xi>0$ gives
$\limsup D_H^*/H^{5/2}\le Fr\eta\sqrt\xi/10$.
Only after this $H$ limit send $\xi\downarrow0$.
The constants may depend on $\xi$; no $\xi=0$ calendar or unrestricted
$\xi(H)$ substitution has been used.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{A fair scaled-memory comparison.}
Take the same plant $A=0,B_u=I_2,B=e_2$.
Compare $K_0=0$ with
$K_{\rm copy}=-N_\gamma$,
$N_\gamma=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&\gamma\\0&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$,
$\gamma=1/2$.
Both have zero poles, gain infinity norm at most $1/2$ and a common
one-step homogeneous contraction bound $1/2$.
For fixed external $Q_\epsilon=\operatorname{diag}(\epsilon,1)$,
use $G=\operatorname{diag}(\sqrt\epsilon,1)$ if $\epsilon>0$ and $G=e_2$ if
$\epsilon=0$. In every case $F=1$ and
\[
\beta_0(k)=k,\qquad
\beta_{\rm copy}(1)=\frac{\epsilon}{\epsilon+\gamma^2},\qquad
\beta_{\rm copy}(2)=\frac{\gamma^2+2\epsilon}{\epsilon+\gamma^2}.
\]
At $\epsilon=1/100,k=1$, the coefficient ratio is $1/\sqrt{26}$.
At $\epsilon=0,k=1$, both inputs are recoverable despite strict norm contraction.
For common exact two-step homogeneous extinction, take $k=2$:
both matrices square to zero and the coefficients are $2$ and $1$.
Gain norms do not imply a common actuator power or hard-command bound.

\paragraph{Mean-mode recovery is weaker than full input recovery.}
In three dimensions, a scaled copy of coordinate two into coordinate one,
with $G=[e_2,e_3]$ and $B=e_2$, recovers the force score but not the preceding
independent third-channel innovation.
A stronger example is the 3D shift with the same $G$ and $B=e_2+e_3$.
Its two-step noise matrix is $[e_1,e_2,e_2,e_3]$, rank three for four latent
inputs. The constant ray is preserved, $\beta_1=0$, but $P_2\ne I_4$ and
$L_{2,2}=1/2$.
An order-$d$ shift with $G=B=e_d$ attains the capacity bound:
$\beta_k=0$ for $k\le d-1$, and $\beta_k=k+1-d$ afterward.
